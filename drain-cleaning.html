Signs of a clog

  • Water draining slowly from a sink, tub or shower
  • Gurgling when a drain runs or a toilet flushes
  • Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains
  • A sewer smell from a drain
  • More than one drain slow at the same time, which points to the main line

What causes clogs in Austin homes

Kitchen clogs are almost always grease and food scraps that harden inside the pipe. Bathroom drains fill up with hair and soap. In older neighborhoods like Allandale, Crestview and Travis Heights, tree roots work their way into clay and cast iron sewer lines. And Austin's hard water leaves mineral scale that narrows pipes over time.

How we clear drains

  1. Find the clog. We ask which drains are slow and test a few to tell if it's one fixture or the main line.
  2. Clear it with the right tool. A cable machine handles most clogs. For heavy grease or roots we use a hydro-jetter that scours the pipe wall clean.
  3. Look inside when it helps. If a clog keeps coming back, we run a camera so you know if it's buildup, roots or a broken section.
  4. Help you keep it clear. Usually that means grease in the trash instead of the sink, and a hair catcher in the shower.

Common questions

Should I try a chemical drain cleaner first?

We'd rather you didn't. Liquid drain openers rarely clear a full clog, they can damage older pipes, and they make the job dangerous for whoever opens the drain next. A plunger is fine. If that doesn't work, give us a call.

What's the difference between snaking and hydro-jetting?

Snaking pushes a spinning cable through the clog and opens a path. Hydro-jetting sprays water at high pressure to clean the whole inside of the pipe. Snaking costs less and works for most sinks. Jetting is better for grease-packed kitchen lines and roots in the main line.

Why does my drain keep clogging?

A clog that comes back within a few months usually means something in the line is catching debris, like a sag, a root or a broken joint. A camera inspection shows exactly what it is, so you're not paying to clear the same clog again and again.
