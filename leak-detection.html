Signs of a hidden leak

  • Your water bill jumped but your habits didn't change
  • The water meter moves with every faucet turned off
  • Warm spots on a tile or concrete floor
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Damp carpet, a musty smell or mold near the bottom of walls
  • New cracks in floors or walls

Why slab leaks are so common here

Most Austin homes sit on a concrete slab poured over clay soil. The clay swells when it's wet and shrinks in a drought, and that movement stresses the water lines under the slab. Hot water lines fail most often, which is why a warm spot on the floor is such a good clue.

Check for a leak yourself

  1. Turn off every faucet, plus the dishwasher, washing machine, ice maker and sprinklers.
  2. Find your water meter, usually in a concrete box near the curb, and lift the lid.
  3. Look at the small triangle or star on the meter face. If it's turning, water is going somewhere.
  4. Write down the reading, wait an hour without using any water, and check it again.

If the numbers changed, call us. A running toilet can do the same thing, so it isn't always a slab leak, but it's worth finding either way.

Common questions

Is a reroute better than breaking the slab?

Often, yes. A reroute abandons the leaking pipe under the slab and runs a new line through the walls and attic. There's no jackhammering, and that section can't leak under the floor again. If the leak is easy to reach or it's a drain line, a spot repair can make more sense.

Will my insurance pay for a slab leak?

Many policies cover the cost of getting to the leak and repairing the damage, but not the pipe repair itself. We give you the detection report, photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

How accurate is leak detection?

We can usually pinpoint a leak to within a foot or two. Louder leaks close to the surface are easiest. We'll tell you how confident we are before anyone starts cutting.
