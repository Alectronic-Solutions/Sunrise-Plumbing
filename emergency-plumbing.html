Reviewed by Marcus Rivera, Texas Master Plumber Updated September 27, 2026

Stop the water first. If water is spreading, shut off the main valve before anything else. In most Austin homes it's in the meter box near the curb or on the pipe where water enters the house, often in the garage. Our emergency guide walks you through it.

Emergencies we handle

  • Burst and frozen pipes
  • Sewage backing up into tubs, showers or floor drains
  • Water heater leaking or spilling from the relief valve
  • Slab leaks with warm spots or water coming up through the floor
  • Toilets that keep overflowing
  • Losing water pressure in the whole house

What happens when you call

  1. We ask a few questions about what you're seeing and whether the water is shut off.
  2. We tell you who's coming and when. Most emergency calls get an arrival time under an hour.
  3. The plumber stops the damage first, then gives you a price for the full repair.
  4. You decide. If you'd rather wait for a scheduled visit, we make it safe and come back.

If you smell gas, leave the house before calling anyone. Call 911 and your gas company from outside, then call us for the repair.

Common questions

Do you really answer at 3 a.m.?

Yes. Our own dispatch staff in Austin answers around the clock. It's not an answering service. If you need a plumber, one is on the way before you hang up.

Is it cheaper to wait until morning?

Not with us. We don't charge more at night, so the only reason to wait is if the problem is small and the water is off. We'll tell you honestly if it can wait.

Will homeowner's insurance cover it?

Often the water damage is covered but the pipe repair itself is not. We give you an itemized invoice and photos of the damage for your claim. Call your insurance company early, since most policies want prompt notice.
